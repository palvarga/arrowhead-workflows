An operator states a production goal, a language model translates it into a ProductionIntent, and a deterministic compiler turns that into an Arrowhead Choreographer plan. Before anything reaches a machine, the plan is checked statically and dry-run on a digital twin of the fourteen workstations; the benchmark oracle then decides whether the plan does what was asked. Pick one of the twelve expert scenarios and a translation: the certified reference plan or one of the plausible wrong ones (traps).

How this page works. The station models are a JavaScript port of the limits and formulas the cell publishes to the plan generator: tool wear = cycle/60 · 300/Vc, stack height = 144 mm + layers · case height, tray mass, coating cure windows, the 12-job booth filter, battery reserves, travel ranges and payload limits. Execution follows the twin's rules: steps in topological order, the plan repeated session-quantity times, station state carried over, stop at the first failing step. Where the published limits give no formula the values are schematic: AMR floor coordinates and energy, drone energy per flight (chosen to match the benchmark note of X05), initial stock (14 per colour), oven ramp slopes, the simulated weighing (nominal + drift), and the default case height (200 mm). It is an illustration and not the cell's own digital twin. The reviewer (L5) and the human gate (L7) are not modelled here.
